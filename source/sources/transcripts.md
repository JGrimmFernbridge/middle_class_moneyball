All right, so what I want to talk about today is the Holy Grail of distribution, something that is oftentimes neglected and not thought about in the way that we should as financial advisors or when people are planning for the future. So where this begins is with an idea from Ray Dalio, one of the most famous hedge fund managers ever, and he talks about the Holy Grail of investing.

Essentially, what this does is says if you have this investment, because you only have it, you carry on a high level of risk with that particular investment. But if you add in another investment that's not correlated as much, then you reduce your risk slightly. Then what happens is if you add a third investment that's not correlated to the mix, you reduce your risk even more so. And lastly, if you were to, in his mind, have between 15 and 20 different non-correlated assets at play, you essentially can reduce your risk to almost zero, or the risk of losing. Because what he's working off here is how do we maintain our returns by lowering our risk? Normally what happens is in order to get high returns, you have to take on more risk, but when you do a strategy and you layer in non-correlated assets together, then you reduce your risk and you keep your returns relatively high.

And where these risk factors oftentimes think of is market risk, how likely the investment is to lose money, and that could be due to a number of different factors, market corrections, a commodity going out. So the goal here is to try and reduce risk because we want to keep our return high and our risk low.

And so what happens then is we want to essentially take what people have done by saving and investing and accumulating, and then at some point, we need to turn that into distribution, whether that's income, buying a property, or saving for a vacation, or in most cases, retirement income is what most of the industry then does. And so then at some time in the future, we take what you've accumulated, and we turn it into distribution. But then that brings about some of the greatest risk factors, and we essentially invite this whole bucket of risk into the equation in a new way that didn't exist in this phase of accumulation. And that's where all the problems actually rest for most people, is that most people have not thought about all of the risks that are associated in this distribution phase.

And most financial advisors don't plan for this phase. They get there through accumulation and then figure it out once they get to this time or close to it. Maybe you meet with your advisor when you're sixty to find out if what you've accumulated will work in distribution, and that's when people start to turn it on.

So what happens at that phase is we've been told our whole lives that accumulation goes to distribution. But I ask the question, does that always work? The other thing that people don't oftentimes think about from accumulation to distribution is that some great distribution products are based off of health, wellness. But then that also usually means we have to get more conservative, and so we have to reduce return in almost all cases because we cannot afford loss. The other thing that we do here is we use, in general terms, something like the 4% rule, a safe withdrawal rate strategy, because we only have a limited amount of funds that we have to try and make last your whole life. And this can range, this can be as high as 5, 10%, depending on what's happening out there in the market. It also might be zero if the market is down and you cannot take your money out when the market is down. And so then that goes into this risk factor of thinking about longevity, thinking about how long can we make this last, what's inflation like out there, are the markets up or down? And so this says we can basically take about this much, 4%, out and make it last. But what happens with both of these rules is advisors get locked into making this turn from here into there, and that's really ineffective for people, especially over the long haul, and especially if they're hoping to try and make their assets last.

So what this got us thinking of is, can we do something different? Can we make a different model that doesn't create tons of risk in distribution, but still gets the accumulation of assets needed for all of life? And so what we started to do was think through the distribution risk, which we now call, to borrow from Mr. Dalio, the Holy Grail of Distribution.

Now, what's great about the Holy Grail of Distribution is it applies the same philosophy of trying to keep returns high and reduce risk.
